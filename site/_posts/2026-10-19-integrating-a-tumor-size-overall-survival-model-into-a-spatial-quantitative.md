### Quick Take
This paper presents a spatial PBPK-QSP framework that couples whole-body ADC disposition, a Krogh-cylinder tumor model, and a published NSCLC TS-OS model. It is essential reading for QSP/PBPK modelers and ADC translational scientists seeking to link mechanistic tumor dynamics to survival endpoints without patient-level tumor data. The framework reproduces PK, ORR, DCR, and OS trends for T-DXd and Dato-DXd with only two fitted parameters.

---

### Executive Summary
The authors developed an integrated spatial QSP platform that mechanistically links ADC and payload PBPK, intratumoral transport in a Krogh cylinder, tumor cell killing via a transit-compartment PD cascade, and overall survival through a published NSCLC TS-OS model. Using a common model structure and virtual-patient framework, with parameters from literature or independent assays and only two estimated clearance parameters, the model simultaneously reproduced serum PK, ORR, DCR, and exploratory OS for trastuzumab deruxtecan in HER2-mutant NSCLC and datopotamab deruxtecan in EGFR-mutated NSCLC. The work demonstrates transportability of a non-ADC TS-OS model to ADC-generated tumor trajectories and supports model-informed dose selection, identifying 5.4 mg/kg Q3W T-DXd and 6 mg/kg Q3W Dato-DXd as balanced benefit-exposure doses.

---

### Scientific Context & Motivation
ADCs in solid tumors face challenges from heterogeneous target expression, limited tumor penetration, and off-target payload exposure. Existing models separate whole-body PBPK from spatial tumor models, and TS-OS survival models typically require patient-level tumor data. This study addresses the gap by embedding a Krogh-cylinder tumor model within a full-body PBPK framework and linking mechanistically generated tumor trajectories to a published NSCLC TS-OS model developed without ADC-treated populations, enabling early-stage survival projections and dose evaluation.

---

## ⚡ Methodological Snapshot
The model integrates a full-body PBPK module for intact ADC, total antibody, and unconjugated payload with a Krogh-cylinder spatial tumor model. The tumor model includes radial diffusion, target binding, internalization, intracellular payload release, bystander killing, and mixed Ag+/Ag− cell populations. A transit-compartment dual PD cascade describes delayed cell killing. Tumor trajectories are linked to OS via a published NSCLC TS-OS model. The system comprises 508 ODEs implemented in SimBiology (MATLAB R2024b). Calibration used particle swarm optimization to estimate two clearance parameters against T-DXd serum PK. Virtual patients were generated by sampling parameters from prespecified distributions with selected correlations.

---

## 🏗️ Structural Model Breakdown
The model comprises: (1) whole-body PBPK with multiple organs (lung, GI, heart, skin, kidney, spleen, brain, etc.), FcRn recycling, pinocytosis, lymphatic drainage, and lysosomal degradation; (2) explicit deconjugation kinetics linking intact ADC to total antibody and unconjugated payload; (3) a Krogh-cylinder tumor compartment with radial discretization, vascular permeability, diffusion, target binding, internalization, intracellular payload release, and bystander effects; (4) mixed Ag+ and Ag− tumor cell populations with transit-compartment dual PD cascade (proliferative, early quiescent, late quiescent, fully quiescent states); (5) TS-OS sub-model linking simulated tumor size trajectories to survival. Total 508 ODEs.

### Detailed Methodological Analysis

#### Modeling Approach
Spatial PBPK-QSP: whole-body PBPK for ADC/antibody/payload disposition coupled to a Krogh-cylinder tumor transport model and transit-compartment PD cascade; TS-OS linkage using Struemper et al. model. Implemented in SimBiology (MATLAB R2024b).

#### Data Sources
Published phase I T-DXd PK data (Doi et al.) at 6.4 mg/kg Q3W (n=6) for calibration; clinical benchmarks for ORR/DCR/OS from DESTINY-Lung01 (T-DXd) and TROPION-PanTumor01 (Dato-DXd). All other parameters from literature or independent assays.

#### Estimation Methods
Particle swarm optimization (PSO) in MATLAB/SimBiology fitting framework; two parameters estimated: nonspecific antibody uptake (CL) and unconjugated payload hepatic clearance (CL_PL).

#### Model Evaluation
Visual comparison of simulated vs observed serum PK; comparison of predicted ORR/DCR and OS curves against clinical benchmarks; VP ensemble simulations (1000-5000 VPs) with median and 95% CI; sensitivity analyses of VP parameters vs response and OS.

#### Covariate Analysis
VP parameter distributions compared between responders and non-responders; associations between tumor growth rate, HSCR, AgPS and response/OS examined. No formal covariate model; VP parameters sampled from correlated distributions.

---

### Statistical Rigor Assessment
The calibration is based on a small PK dataset (n=6) and only two parameters are estimated, so full model identifiability is not claimed. VP simulations use 1000-5000 virtual patients with repeated sampling to quantify cohort-level uncertainty. ORR/DCR are compared as population-level benchmarks, not fitted. The TS-OS model is applied with an adjusted dispersion parameter. Sensitivity analyses are descriptive rather than formal global sensitivity analyses. Missing individual-level tumor and survival data limit validation.

## 📊 Key Findings
The calibrated model captured T-DXd serum PK (intact ADC, total antibody, unconjugated payload) with only two fitted parameters: nonspecific antibody uptake (CL) and unconjugated payload hepatic clearance (CL_PL). Simulated ORR and DCR matched clinical benchmarks for both ADCs. Predicted median OS was consistent with DESTINY-Lung01 and TROPION-PanTumor01. Tumor growth rate was the strongest survival-associated input; HSCR and AgPS were associated with response but with overlapping responder/non-responder distributions. Dose-response analysis showed diminishing efficacy gains at higher doses: T-DXd ORR increased from 31% at 4.4 mg/kg to 48% at 5.4 mg/kg and 56% at 6.4 mg/kg, with median OS 14.0, 16.9, and 17.8 months, while payload AUC increased ~23% and ~45%. Dato-DXd ORR increased from 24% at 4 mg/kg to 41% at 6 mg/kg and 51% at 8 mg/kg, with median OS 12.0, 16.3, and 17.0 months, while payload AUC increased ~50% and ~100%. The model supports 5.4 mg/kg Q3W T-DXd and 6 mg/kg Q3W Dato-DXd.

---

## 💡 Clinical & Regulatory Implications
The framework supports model-informed dose selection by balancing efficacy (ORR, OS) against unconjugated payload exposure as a safety surrogate. It identifies 5.4 mg/kg Q3W T-DXd and 6 mg/kg Q3W Dato-DXd as balanced doses, consistent with approved doses. It also highlights the need for multidimensional biomarkers combining tumor growth rate, HSCR, and AgPS to predict response. The platform can be used to explore ADC design levers (DAR, linker, payload) and to generate hypotheses for future clinical validation.

### Strengths & Limitations

#### Strengths
- First framework linking whole-body ADC PBPK, spatial Krogh-cylinder tumor model, and OS in a single structure
- Only two parameters estimated from serum PK; all other parameters from literature or independent assays
- Common model structure and virtual-patient framework successfully applied to two ADCs with distinct targets
- Reproduces PK, ORR, DCR, and OS trends without fitting individual tumor trajectories or adding ad hoc response compartments
- Provides a practical demonstration of TS-OS model transportability to ADC-generated tumor trajectories
- Model-informed dose selection framework jointly evaluates efficacy and exposure-based safety surrogates

#### Limitations (Acknowledged by Authors)
- Tumor burden represented as a single lumped lesion without lesion-level heterogeneity or new lesions
- ORR/DCR evaluated at fixed 12-month landmark rather than RECIST-based BOR
- Toxicity not modeled mechanistically; payload AUC used as exposure-based surrogate
- Predicted human tissue profiles not validated against clinical tissue data
- TMDD not included; target expression in healthy tissues omitted
- Obesity-related physiological differences not represented (proportional scaling of all tissues with body weight)
- TS-OS application required adjustment of survival dispersion parameter
- OS predictions are empirical translational projections, not mechanistic survival model

#### Limitations (Expert Review)
- Only two parameters estimated; identifiability of the full 508-ODE system is not established
- Use of T-DXd PK data from non-NSCLC patients (breast/gastric) to inform NSCLC exposure may introduce bias
- Common DXd payload clearance assumed for both ADCs, potentially affecting payload exposure predictions
- Responder/non-responder overlap indicates limited standalone predictive value of individual biomarkers
- Fixed spatial parameters (capillary radius, diffusivity) across virtual patients may underestimate inter-patient variability in tumor physiology
- Simulations of 5000 VPs with repeated sampling may not fully capture parameter uncertainty propagation

#### Generalizability
The framework is designed to be transferable across ADC formats and targets, as demonstrated by application to T-DXd and Dato-DXd. However, generalizability to other tumor types, payloads, or combination regimens requires re-parameterization and validation. The TS-OS linkage is specific to NSCLC and may not extrapolate to other indications.

### Future Directions
Future work should evaluate the framework using matched individual-level PK, biomarker, longitudinal tumor-size, treatment, safety, and survival data. Incorporating prior treatment, performance status, inflammatory markers, and tumor-burden biomarkers may improve patient heterogeneity representation. Extensions could include explicit toxicity modules (QST), immune components, lesion-level dynamics, and multidimensional biomarker identification.

---

### Expert Commentary
This paper represents a significant step toward integrating mechanistic spatial tumor models with clinically meaningful survival endpoints. The use of a published non-ADC TS-OS model to translate QSP-generated tumor trajectories is pragmatic and addresses a common translational bottleneck. However, the reliance on aggregate clinical benchmarks and the small number of fitted parameters means the model should be viewed as a hypothesis-generating platform rather than a validated predictive tool. The dose-selection framework is particularly useful, as it explicitly quantifies the trade-off between efficacy gains and payload exposure. The next frontier is prospective validation with individual-level data and incorporation of toxicity and immune modules.

---

### Bottom Line
This study provides a comprehensive spatial PBPK-QSP platform that bridges ADC systemic disposition, intratumoral transport, tumor response, and survival. For practicing pharmacometricians, the key takeaway is that a mechanistically grounded, spatially resolved tumor model can generate clinically consistent tumor trajectories and OS projections without patient-level tumor data, and can support dose selection by balancing efficacy against payload exposure. The framework is a valuable template for early ADC development, though prospective validation with matched individual-level data is needed.
